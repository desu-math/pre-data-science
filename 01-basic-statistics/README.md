# 📊 Basic Statistics

### Understanding Data Through Statistical Thinking

## 1. Introduction

How can we make meaningful conclusions from a collection of data? How can we summarize large datasets, identify patterns, and make informed decisions based on observations?

**Statistics provides the mathematical tools and reasoning needed to answer these questions.**

Statistics is a branch of mathematics concerned with collecting, organizing, summarizing, analyzing, and interpreting data. It helps us understand information, identify patterns, evaluate uncertainty, and make decisions based on evidence.

In Data Science, statistics plays a fundamental role in understanding datasets, discovering relationships between variables, evaluating models, and drawing conclusions from data.

This section develops the fundamental concepts of statistics, starting with basic ideas and progressively introducing the mathematical methods needed for data analysis.

Our focus is not simply to learn statistical formulas. Instead, we aim to understand the ideas behind them, why they work, how they are derived, and when they should be used.

---

## 2. Learning Objectives

By completing this section, learners should be able to:

1. Understand fundamental statistical terminology and concepts.
2. Identify different types of data and variables.
3. Understand populations, samples, and sampling methods.
4. Organize and present data effectively.
5. Calculate and interpret measures of central tendency.
6. Understand and calculate measures of dispersion.
7. Describe the position and distribution of observations.
8. Explore relationships between variables.
9. Understand the fundamental ideas of statistical inference.
10. Apply selected statistical methods using Python.
11. Interpret statistical results in practical data analysis problems.

## 3. Learning Roadmap

The subject is organized into the following major areas.

### Part I: Introduction to Statistics and Data

This part establishes the basic language and ideas of statistics.

Topics include:

- Meaning and importance of Statistics.
- Descriptive and Inferential Statistics.
- Population and Sample.
- Statistical Units and Observations.
- Variables and Types of Data.
- Qualitative and Quantitative Variables.
- Discrete and Continuous Variables.
- Sources and Methods of Data Collection.

**Learning outcome:** Understand what statistics studies, what data represents, and how statistical information is obtained.

### Part II: Data Organization and Presentation

How can we transform raw data into information that is easier to understand?

This part introduces methods of organizing and presenting data.

Topics include:

- Classification and Organization of Data.
- Frequency Distribution Tables.
- Relative and Cumulative Frequencies.
- Graphical Presentation of Data.
- Bar Charts and Pie Charts.
- Histograms.
- Frequency Polygons.
- Ogives.

**Learning outcome:** Organize datasets and communicate statistical information using appropriate tables and graphical methods.

### Part III: Measures of Central Tendency

How can we represent a dataset using a single value that describes its center?

This part introduces measures that describe the central position of observations.

Topics include:

- Arithmetic Mean.
- Weighted Mean.
- Median.
- Mode.
- Relationships Between Measures of Central Tendency.
- Choosing an Appropriate Measure.

**Learning outcome:** Calculate, compare, and interpret different measures of central tendency.

### Part IV: Measures of Dispersion

Two datasets may have the same average but very different distributions. How can we describe their differences?

This part introduces statistical measures that describe the spread or variability of data.

Topics include:

- Range.
- Interquartile Range.
- Quartile Deviation.
- Mean Absolute Deviation.
- Variance.
- Standard Deviation.
- Coefficient of Variation.

**Learning outcome:** Measure and interpret variability, understand the importance of dispersion, and compare datasets.

### Part V: Measures of Position and Distribution

How can we determine the relative position of an observation within a dataset?

This part introduces methods of describing the position and shape of data distributions.

Topics include:

- Quartiles.
- Deciles.
- Percentiles.
- Standard Scores (Z-scores).
- Skewness.
- Introduction to Kurtosis.

**Learning outcome:** Understand the relative position of observations and describe important characteristics of data distributions.

### Part VI: Relationship Between Variables

How can we determine whether two variables are related?

This part introduces basic methods for studying relationships between variables.

Topics include:

- Bivariate Data.
- Scatter Plots.
- Covariance.
- Correlation.
- Pearson's Correlation Coefficient.
- Introduction to Simple Linear Regression.

**Learning outcome:** Identify, measure, and interpret relationships between variables.

### Part VII: Introduction to Statistical Inference

Can we use information from a sample to make conclusions about an entire population?

This part introduces the fundamental ideas of statistical inference.

Topics include:

- Population Parameters and Sample Statistics.
- Sampling Variability.
- Point Estimation.
- Introduction to Confidence Intervals.
- Introduction to Hypothesis Testing.
- Statistical Significance.

**Learning outcome:** Understand how statistical information from samples can be used to make conclusions about populations.

*Note: Probability theory provides the mathematical foundation for many inferential methods. Detailed probability concepts and their mathematical development are covered separately in the Basic Probability section.*

## 5. Learning Methodology

We follow a concept-oriented and problem-solving approach.

Our learning process generally follows these stages:

1. **Question:** Introduce a meaningful question that motivates the topic.
2. **Intuition:** Develop an intuitive understanding of the underlying idea.
3. **Definition:** Establish precise mathematical definitions.
4. **Mathematical Development:** Explain important properties and derive relevant formulas.
5. **Worked Examples:** Apply the concepts to carefully selected problems.
6. **Interpretation:** Explain what the mathematical results mean.
7. **Implementation:** Translate selected methods into algorithms and Python programs.
8. **Application:** Connect the concepts with practical data analysis and Data Science.

We prioritize clarity and mathematical understanding over unnecessary complexity.

Not every statistical method requires a Python implementation, and not every advanced statistical concept needs to be introduced at this foundational level. We select materials according to their educational importance and relevance to Data Science.
